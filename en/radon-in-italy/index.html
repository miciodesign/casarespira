Radon in Italy: what foreign home buyers and tenants should know

In short

  • Italy sets 300 Bq/m³ (existing homes) and 200 Bq/m³ (homes permitted after 2024) as annual-average reference levels; the WHO recommends 100.
  • Sellers and landlords are not required to test or disclose radon: test before the deed, with a laboratory measurement of several months.
  • Priority areas are published by each region, but high radon is found outside them too.

Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that rises from the ground and collects in basements and ground floors. Italy's estimated national average is around 70 Bq/m³, with wide regional variation (100-120 in Lombardy, according to a 2025 ATS Brescia presentation); it has a specific law, and no obligation for a seller or landlord to test. If you are buying a farmhouse in Piedmont, a flat in Milan or a villa on a lake, this is what you need to know.

The legal reference levels

Italian Legislative Decree 101/2020, which implements the Euratom directive 2013/59, sets reference levels for the annual average radon concentration in air (article 12):

  • 300 Bq/m³ for existing homes and for workplaces;
  • 200 Bq/m³ for homes built under permits issued after 31 December 2024.

The World Health Organization recommends 100 Bq/m³. A reference level is not a safety threshold: above it, the law expects remediation; below it, lower is still better.

Priority areas

Article 11 of the decree asks each region to identify "priority areas": municipalities where more than 15% of buildings are estimated to exceed 300 Bq/m³. The National Radon Action Plan 2023-2032 (approved in January 2024) set the method. Lombardy was first, with 90 municipalities; by early 2026 several regions had published their lists (Lombardy, Liguria, Aosta Valley, the autonomous provinces of Bolzano and Trento, among others), for a total of several hundred municipalities. Lists are updated: check the region's or the regional environmental agency's (ARPA) website for the municipality you are looking at. Our Italian page on Lombardy explains how the areas were chosen.

Being outside a priority area does not mean low radon. ARPA Lombardy says it plainly: high concentrations are found outside the mapped municipalities too. Only a measurement tells you.

Why the seller will not tell you

Italian law does not require a private seller or landlord to measure radon or to disclose a measurement. The energy performance certificate (APE) that every sale and lease must include says nothing about radon. Radon clauses in preliminary contracts are rare. So the burden is on you, and the time to act is before the deed (rogito), not after.

How to get a measurement

  • What counts is the annual average. A reading taken in one afternoon with a consumer device is not a legal measurement. The reference method is a passive detector left in place for months and analysed by a laboratory.
  • Who does it. A recognised dosimetry service or an ARPA-type laboratory. Ask for a report that states the measurement period, the room, the floor and the method (the technical standard is UNI ISO 11665-8).
  • Timing for a purchase. If you have months before the deed, place detectors now; a winter measurement of at least three months is far more informative than nothing. If you have weeks, an electronic monitor gives you a trend and a first warning, and you can write a radon condition into the preliminary contract, with a price adjustment or a right to withdraw above an agreed level.
  • Where to measure. The lowest lived-in floor, with the house used normally, windows closed as in daily life.

Our Italian guide Come si misura il radon has the details; the full guide covers remediation.

If the result is high

Radon can be reduced. The most common technique in houses on a concrete slab is soil depressurisation: a sump under or beside the building with a fan that draws the gas from the ground and vents it outside. Costs depend on the building; we publish them only when verified. For new builds, Italian regional guidelines recommend a ventilated crawl space (vespaio aerato) with a radon membrane and a passive sump that can take a fan later; see our page on the vespaio aerato (in Italian).

Questions to ask your notary, surveyor or agent

  • Is the municipality in a regional radon priority area?
  • Has the property ever been measured? Can I see the report?
  • Does the ground floor sit on a ventilated crawl space, a slab on grade, or a basement?
  • If the building was permitted after 31 December 2024: what radon-proofing was designed in?
  • Can we add a radon condition to the preliminary contract (compromesso)?

This page describes the Italian rules as published; it is not legal, technical or medical advice. For a specific property, rely on a qualified surveyor (geometra or engineer) and, for health questions, on a doctor.
