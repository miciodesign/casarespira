Air and water due diligence for property in Italy

In short

  • Radon testing is mandatory in underground workplaces, in ground-floor and semi-basement workplaces inside regional priority areas, and in listed activities (Decree 101/2020, art. 16-17).
  • Water inside buildings is the owner's responsibility from the meter; lead drops to 5 µg/L in 2036 and priority buildings must manage Legionella and lead risk (Decree 18/2023).
  • Mould and ventilation: no legal limits in private buildings, but ISS and Ministry of Health benchmarks exist (1,000 ppm CO₂, 50% humidity).

Environmental due diligence on Italian property usually covers soil, asbestos and energy performance. Two items are often missed because the rules are recent: radon in workplaces, which became a legal obligation for employers with Legislative Decree 101/2020, and drinking water inside buildings, reorganised by Legislative Decree 18/2023. This page lists what to check and where the obligations sit.

Radon: where testing is mandatory

Article 16 of Decree 101/2020 applies the radon rules to these workplaces:

  • underground workplaces (rooms with at least three walls fully below ground);
  • workplaces in semi-basements or on the ground floor located in a regional priority area;
  • specific activities listed by the National Radon Action Plan (for example water treatment plants with open tanks, mineral-water bottling, hydroelectric plants);
  • spas.

Article 17 puts the obligation on the operator (esercente, in practice the employer or occupier): complete a measurement of the annual average concentration within 24 months of starting the activity, or, for ground-floor premises in a priority area, within 18 months of the area being published in the Official Gazette. The measurement must be done by a recognised dosimetry service and becomes part of the risk assessment document under the health-and-safety code. Above 300 Bq/m³ the operator must reduce the concentration within two years with a certified radon remediation expert, then re-measure. INAIL, the national accident insurance institute, has published a guide for employers.

What this means for an acquisition. If the asset has ground-floor or basement space used as workplaces, in a priority area or in one of the listed activities, ask for the radon measurement reports and the risk assessment. Their absence is a compliance gap and a possible capital expense; a result above 300 Bq/m³ is a remediation obligation that transfers with the occupier. Priority-area lists are regional and still being completed: the regional environmental agency (ARPA) is the source.

Drinking water inside buildings

Decree 18/2023, implementing EU directive 2020/2184, moved from end-of-pipe compliance to risk management along the whole supply chain. The water utility is responsible up to the meter; from the meter to the tap, responsibility lies with the owner or the building manager. Two points matter for buildings:

  • Priority buildings (hospitals, care homes, hotels, schools, sports facilities, large public buildings and similar) must assess and manage the risk of their internal distribution systems, with specific attention to Legionella and lead.
  • Lead. The parameter value is 10 µg/L today and drops to 5 µg/L on 12 January 2036. Buildings with lead pipes or fittings, typically built up to the early 1980s, will need pipe replacement to comply. Ask for the age and material of risers and a recent analysis at the tap.

PFAS limits also apply at the tap from 2026 (sum of PFAS 0.10 µg/L); they concern the utility's water, but in areas with known contamination (parts of Veneto, and in 2026 a case in Liguria) they affect how a building is supplied. Our Italian guides on tap water and PFAS have the details.

Mould, damp and ventilation

Italy has no indoor air-quality limits for private buildings, but the National Institute of Health (ISS) uses 1,000 ppm of CO₂ as the threshold above which ventilation is insufficient, and the Ministry of Health recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 50% to prevent mould. For a portfolio, the practical checks are: evidence of mould or rising damp on ground floors; whether the ground floor sits on a ventilated crawl space (vespaio aerato) or a slab in contact with the ground; and, in buildings renovated for energy efficiency, whether ventilation was addressed, because tighter envelopes without ventilation are the typical cause of new mould problems. Mechanical ventilation with heat recovery qualifies for energy tax deductions only under conditions set by ENEA.

A checklist

  1. Municipality in a regional radon priority area? (ARPA)
  2. Any underground, semi-basement or ground-floor workplaces? Radon reports and risk assessment available?
  3. Building type under Decree 18/2023: priority building? Water risk assessment done?
  4. Age and material of water risers; last analysis at the tap (lead, Legionella where relevant).
  5. Ground-floor construction: crawl space, slab, basement; radon membrane in new builds.
  6. Visible mould or damp; ventilation strategy after any energy retrofit.

This page summarises Italian rules as published and is not legal or technical advice. Obligations depend on the use, the location and the region; engage an Italian environmental consultant and a qualified radiation-protection or radon expert for a specific asset.
